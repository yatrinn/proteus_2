01The problem

Confined spaces still need people to climb in.

In process plants, people climb into vessels, columns and heat exchangers to inspect, retrieve and repair. Every entry means a shutdown, a permit, gas tests and a standby attendant at the opening.

Robots that fit through a nozzle can look around. A slender arm or a drone cannot brace itself, so it runs out of force and stiffness at reach. That is the gap Proteus closes.

02How it works

Enter as a chain. Work as a structure.

One chain goes in. One new dock turns it into a structure that can push, hold and carry.

  1. 01

    Enter

    The modules pass the Ø200 mm opening as one docked chain, pushed from outside. The last head latches into the rim.

  2. 02

    Brace

    The last module swings up, extends its head and latches onto the chain. One new dock closes a triangle.

  3. 03

    Work

    The tool load now runs along the modules into the rim and the floor, instead of bending the joints.

ThenTo leave, it opens that one dock, folds back into a chain and withdraws through the same opening.

Why a structure beats a chain

A chain holds a load at its tip by bending, through joints a few centimeters across. By our estimate, today's 50 N actuators could not even hold one of our modules out horizontally as a free chain.

A braced triangle carries the same load along its members into two supports. The joints no longer fight the lever, so the same modules get far stronger and stiffer.

Stage 1 target

At least 10× the force and 50× the stiffness of the same modules as a free chain, and 5× the force of the best supported chain.

Next: we measure it with a printed triangle.

03Platform

One module. Many shapes.

Every Proteus robot is built from the same module: a rigid body with an articulated head at each end. Heads dock face to face. A robot changes shape by changing which faces are docked, one dock at a time.

Snake18 modules

To get in.

When
Pipes, nozzles and ducts: wherever the way in is long and narrow.
Why
A chain passes any opening larger than a single module.
How
Follow the leader: every module passes exactly where the first one did. The wall guides the chain, the push comes from outside.

Walker14 modules

To get around.

When
Crossing the floor of a vessel, a tank or a plant.
Why
Legs step over welds, debris and internals that stop wheels.
How
A rigid frame on four legs. One leg moves at a time while three stay down, and the body shifts to keep its weight inside them.

Structure23 modules

To carry the work.

When
Holding tools, carrying loads, bridging gaps.
Why
In a truss, every module carries load along its length, in tension or compression, instead of bending.
How
Diagonals latch onto the top and bottom faces of the chords, end posts onto anchor plates. Here the truss spans more than 3 m and carries a hanging test load.

  • Latched faces

    Modules connect face to face on square faces. Yellow marks a latched dock.

  • One dock at a time

    A dock opens only when the rest of the structure holds the load.

  • Always stable

    A walker keeps three feet on the ground. A structure stands on its supports.

05Market

Start where people still climb in. Grow into a platform.

First

Process plants

Chemical, refining and energy plants are full of vessels, columns, heat exchangers and tanks with small nozzles. Inspection, retrieval and small repairs behind those nozzles are our first jobs.

Then

A platform

Modules, docks, tools and software that turn the same parts into the structure each job needs. Every new tool and every new structure works with the modules a customer already owns.

Later

Critical infrastructure, disaster response and decommissioning, wherever a person would otherwise have to go in. Long term, assembly and servicing in orbit.

The goal

Dangerous work, done by machines that build themselves around the job.
